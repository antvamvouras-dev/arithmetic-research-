# DHMC-004 — Results

## Main result
The residual survives both richer deterministic wheel baselines on all four chronological windows, but shrinks substantially as the wheel becomes richer.

### Held-out gains
| window | modulus | wheel gain | residual gain over wheel |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 210 | 0.562411 | 0.020982 |
| 2 | 210 | 0.548462 | 0.022515 |
| 3 | 210 | 0.545652 | 0.024190 |
| 4 | 210 | 0.544971 | 0.022623 |
| 1 | 2310 | 0.672770 | 0.010805 |
| 2 | 2310 | 0.657502 | 0.007018 |
| 3 | 2310 | 0.654105 | 0.006287 |
| 4 | 2310 | 0.652729 | 0.006081 |

Across windows:
- `m=210`: mean wheel gain `0.550374`; mean residual `0.022578`; positive in `4/4` windows.
- `m=2310`: mean wheel gain `0.659277`; mean residual `0.007548`; positive in `4/4` windows.

The residual therefore does not disappear at mod 210 or mod 2310, but the move from mod 30 to mod 2310 absorbs a large fraction of it.

## Survival of previously dominant mod-30 contexts
All 12 dominant contexts from DHMC-003 remain positive after the mod-210 baseline.

After the mod-2310 baseline, 11 of 12 remain positive. The exception is `(g=4, r30=23)`, whose pooled residual becomes slightly negative (`sum K ≈ -6.26`, mean window K ≈ -0.000713`).

Examples after mod 2310:
- `(6,29)`: pooled sum K `270.425`, positive.
- `(6,17)`: `170.424`, positive.
- `(12,13)`: `165.332`, positive.
- `(2,13)`: `118.283`, positive.

## Concentration
Projected back to `(g, r mod 30)` space, the residual remains heterogeneous.

For mod 210, effective mod-30 context count is roughly `34–37`; top 5% of contexts account for about `38–40%` of absolute residual contribution.

For mod 2310, effective mod-30 context count is roughly `29–30`; top 5% account for about `42–43%`, and top 10% about `67–69%`.

Thus the residual becomes smaller under richer arithmetic correction, but does not become diffuse. It remains concentrated in a structured subset of contexts.

## Sparsity warning
At `m=2310`, the training model has roughly `4950–6148` observed `(gap,residue)` contexts per window, and the final test contains roughly `1236–1664` transitions whose exact learned context was unseen in training. These transitions fall back to the deterministic wheel exactly. Therefore the positive `m=2310` residual is not evidence that a fully calibrated 2310-state model has been achieved.

## Current defensible conclusion
A finite wheel explains an increasing fraction of the predictive structure as the modulus is enriched from 30 to 210 to 2310. Nevertheless, a small positive held-out residual remains in all four windows even relative to the mod-2310 deterministic admissibility baseline. Most dominant mod-30 contexts retain positive residual contribution. This residual is a target for stronger controls, not yet a new arithmetic law.

## Next test
The next experiment should separate two possibilities:
1. the remaining residual is generated by finite-sieve information not represented by endpoint wheel admissibility alone (for example local interior divisibility patterns / exact finite-sieve neighborhood constraints), or
2. a genuine sequential interaction remains after those stronger deterministic controls.

A good next baseline is therefore an explicit local finite-sieve transition score that conditions not only on endpoint admissibility but on the complete interior wheel pattern between consecutive prime candidates.
